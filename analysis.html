Almanac 🔗 Analysis HQ 🔗

Clear numbers, plainly graded

Every football game the Almanac prices gets a chance for a home win, a draw and an away win. The biggest of the three is the top pick, and its chance is the confidence. This page puts the two side by side: what the model said before kick-off, and what happened. If calls quoted at 70% land about 70% of the time, the numbers can be trusted at face value. If they don't, this is where it shows.

How a prediction is made

Football runs through one engine, engine.py, in seven stages. Nothing here is a black box: each number on a fixture row comes from a function you can read, and the settings below are read from the live build, not typed in.

Why it works this way

Built in on purpose

  • Ratings, not points tallies. Points already encode wins, goal difference uses an arbitrary scale, and neither is a probability. Attack and defence ratings feed a score model that produces probabilities directly.
  • Last season, then this season. Early in a season a few results say very little, so the prior carries the rating until enough games are in.
  • Calibration after the model. Raw Poisson output is over-confident at the bottom and cautious at the top. A two-number curve corrects it, refitted weekly and only shipped when it also improves a season it was not fitted on.
  • The model's own read only. No bookmaker prices go into any prediction. That keeps the list independent, at a known cost: see the next panel.

Tested and left out

  • Recent form beyond a small capped nudge, last season's head-to-head, rating drift, games played, separate home and away ratings, rest days and per-band temperatures all add nothing measurable once the model's own probability is known.
  • The one signal that does add something is bookmaker agreement, about seven times the rest combined. It carries team news the model cannot see, and it is deliberately not used.
  • Every change has to beat the live model on fit, confirm on the current season, and clear gates: 250+ check games, a paired bootstrap with p(worse) at most 0.30, and a minimum gain in log loss.

How it is graded

Only prices published before kick-off count. Each morning's predictions are archived as they were published, with the publish time recorded since 2 October. Results are matched back on both team names. A price that appeared after kick-off is never graded, and nothing is ever fitted on this record.

Won and read right. The model almost never picks a draw, because a draw is rarely the single likeliest result. So "won" is strict: the top pick happened. "Read right" adds games where the predicted scoreline was a draw and the game ended level. That is a correct read of the match, so the headline record uses it. Its quoted side adds the draw chance on those games, so a band's quoted and landed figures stay comparable.

Bets are different. On the Odds tab a group is a bet, so a draw still loses a win leg there.

Where the results come from. openfootball fills in results a few days after the whistle. ESPN covers the gap and the competitions openfootball does not. Results are kept once settled, so the record never forgets a game.

What it cannot see

Team news. Injuries, rotation, a new manager or a side with nothing to play for are not in the data. That is most of what a person adds when checking a game by hand, and most of the remaining misses.

Blind spots, flagged rather than hidden. Promoted sides, unrated clubs and cross-division cup ties are flagged (Celtic's Law) and dropped a confidence tier. They never make the Daily List.

Small samples at the top. Calls at 85% and above are rare, so one or two results move a band by ten points or more. Read those rows as direction, not proof.

Roughly one Daily List pick in five or six still misses. That is sport, and a higher bar does not remove it.
