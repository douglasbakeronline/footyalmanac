# The List card and the pattern review behind it, 17 November 2026

Douglas, 01:09 on 17 Nov, after a slip built from Daily List picks won at
5.14: "there's got to be a way where we can have at least one or two cards a
day just made up of the daily list picks ... is it a case of just being
random? Is it a case of only picking home teams? ... can you please do a
review of patterns of winners in the daily list ... just having no cards is
awful."

## The pattern review

The results ledger at 17 Nov: 592 graded Daily List picks, 518 landed
(87.5%), 239 with a pre-start bookmaker price. Landed against quoted, and
return at the price where there was one, in every cut that could be made:

| cut | finding |
|---|---|
| by sport | football 84% v 82% quoted; college basketball 94% v 92%; tennis 81% v 83%; rugby 31 of 32; NBA 14 of 15. Priced return: football -0.5%, college basketball -4.1%, NBA +3.8% (13 picks) |
| by side | football list picks are home picks by construction (157 of 164); tennis home and away land alike |
| by confidence band | 75-80% 76.5%, 80-85% 84%, 85-90% 91%, 90-95% 95%, 95%+ 98%: each within 3 points of its quote |
| by price band | 1.00-1.10 95% landed, -2.6%; 1.10-1.20 85%, -2.8%; 1.20-1.35 81%, +1.9% (32 picks) |
| football by family | wider leagues 81.5% v 81.2%; internationals 18 of 18 |
| by weekday | every day within 1.5 standard errors of quote; Monday +17% on 15 priced picks is noise |
| by UK start window | afternoon 80% v 82%; evening 87% v 85% (+2.6% on 70 priced); overnight 93% v 90% |
| model v price | model under the price's implied chance: 93% landed, -2.7%; within 5 points: 90%, +0.6% |

Conclusion: it is not random and it is not a home-team trick. The list
lands as quoted in every cut, which is what a calibrated model does, and no
cut beats the price with any confidence. The winning Douglas sees is the
list picking short favourites that win; what a slip of them pays is set by
the margin on each leg, not by a pattern. The only lever is price: picks at
1.20-1.35 and evening starts run a point or two ahead, on samples too small
to call.

## Why there were no cards

The Card's rule (the model at or above the bookmakers, a priced opposition,
5 legs at most) is a value rule the Daily List's picks mostly fail, because
the list's picks are priced 1.05-1.15. The List 2x on paper (16 Nov) asked
for legs at 1.15+ in six, which many days cannot reach. On the ledger's 23
days with priced list picks: a List 2x at 1.15+ could be built on 5 (all 5
won, +119%, 3.6 legs on average); a List long at any price in up to ten on
7 (3 won, -1.2%, 5.3 legs); the two together on 5 (the long never adds a
day the 2x has). Small numbers, not evidence; the shape is right.

## What shipped

`groupings.LIST_KINDS` now has two kinds, built in order and never both on
one day: the **List 2x** (legs at 1.15+, up to six) and, when it cannot be
made, the **List long** (any price, up to ten). College sports are left out
(`LIST_SPORTS_OFF`: rarely priced in the UK, under quote away). Each group
carries `tested`, the chance it lands on the ledger's landed rates by sport
and price band (`slip.band_rates`), shown beside the model's figure. The
Bets tab shows them in the open as **The List card** (was a fold "on
paper"); the record keeps the List 2x and the List long on their own lines
(`cardList`, `cardListLong`). Staking is Douglas's; nothing is pushed.

## What would change it

- If the List 2x reaches 30 settled groups positive at the archived price
  with day-resampled p(loss) under 0.30, it is a method and gets a stake
  line like the Card. If the List long reaches 30 and sits under the stake,
  it is dropped and the day stays empty.
- Nothing here feeds a model or the Daily List.
